# Find the lowest daylight low

The best low tides for tide pooling are rare and short, and many of them fall in the dark. Slackwater is a free tide app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch that shows you which lows are worth getting up for, and whether the sun will be up when the water is out.

[Download on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/slackwater-tides-current/id6813654837)

## The fortnight's lowest, marked

Tap the Range tile under any tide and Slackwater sets that low among the tides around it. When nothing within fifteen days either side goes lower, it says so: the lowest low of the fortnight. When something does, it tells you how many days until the next lower low, and tapping that fact moves the timeline there.

The Moon card names the cause. Spring tides at the new and full moon widen the range, and when the Moon is also near perigee, its closest point to Earth, the app calls it a perigean spring tide and explains why the lows go lower.

## Is it in daylight?

Sunrise and sunset sit on the same timeline as the tide, so a low at 5:40am with sunrise at 7:05 reads at a glance. Drag along the week to find the low that lands in the middle of the day, and the list underneath gives every high and low for the week.

## How low is low?

At stations whose predictions include the yearly tidal cycle, the Range sheet also tells you how far the low sits above the lowest water that station ever sees. Stations without that cycle in their data, including Canadian stations, leave the line out rather than guess.

## Plan it ahead

With Premium, Slackwater can remind you before a low tide, or only before each day's lowest low, and put a station's highs and lows on your calendar beside the weekend you're planning.

## At the beach, with no signal

Bundled stations predict on your phone for any date, so the answer is there at the end of a dead-end road with no bars. Canadian stations need one download while you have a connection, then work offline. A free home screen widget shows the next high or low and today's curve without opening the app.

## Respect the shore

- **Know when it turns.** Check the time of the next high before you walk out, and head back early. On a flat shore the water comes back faster than it looks.
- **Step on bare rock.** Barnacles, mussels and anemones are alive underfoot, and sand around a pool hides more.
- **Look, then put it back.** Turn a rock over gently and set it down the way you found it. Leave animals and shells in their pools.
- **Harvesting shellfish needs a licence.** In British Columbia that comes from Fisheries and Oceans Canada, and in Washington from the Department of Fish and Wildlife. Check closures for biotoxins and pollution before you dig, every time.

Predictions are not observations. Wind and pressure can hold water in or push it out, and Slackwater is not for navigation.
